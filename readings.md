# Statistical Concepts & Readings

A collection of intuitive summaries on subtle statistical paradoxes and common empirical pitfalls.

---

### 1. Table 2 Fallacy
When fitting a multivariable regression model, coefficients for secondary control variables cannot be interpreted as causal effects in the same way as the primary exposure variable.
* **Key Takeaway:** Adjusting for a variable blocks confounding, but its regression coefficient may still be biased by unmeasured conditioning or colliders.

### 2. Simpson's Paradox
A trend appearing in several groups of data can reverse when the groups are combined.
* **Classic Example:** In cricket, Player A can have a higher overall batting average than Player B across two seasons, yet Player B can have a better average in *both* individual seasons due to sample size imbalances per season.

### 3. Regression Fallacy
Attributing a casual explanation to a purely statistical phenomenon: extreme measurements naturally tend to be closer to the mean on subsequent measurements.
